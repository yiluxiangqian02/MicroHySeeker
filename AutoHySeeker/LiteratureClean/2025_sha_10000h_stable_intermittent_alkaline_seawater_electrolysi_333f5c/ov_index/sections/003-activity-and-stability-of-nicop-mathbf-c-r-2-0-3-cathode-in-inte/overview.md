Section Positioning: This section evaluates the hydrogen evolution reaction (HER) activity and stability of the NiCoP–Cr₂O₃ cathode under intermittent and fluctuating operational conditions relevant to renewable-energy-driven seawater electrolysis.

Paragraph Index:
P001: Describes initial electrochemical evaluation of NiCoP–Cr₂O₃ in alkaline media using a three-electrode system and reports high-performance metrics in an AEM electrolyser configuration.
P002: Presents Figure 2, which compiles HER polarization data, overpotential comparisons across electrolytes, and multiple intermittent stability tests under varied current densities and cycling protocols in seawater-containing electrolytes.
P003: Reports extended stable operation of an AEM electrolyser at 1 A cm⁻² for 140 h under fluctuating power input with negligible voltage decay.
P004: Details HER performance and long-term intermittent stability of NiCoP–Cr₂O₃ in simulated seawater electrolytes (NaCl-spiked and real Yellow Sea water), highlighting low overpotential and minimal activity loss over 300 h.
P005: Demonstrates practical viability through long-duration start–shutdown cycling tests of a full electrolysis cell, showing ultra-low decay rates and superior stability compared to a NiCo–LDH cathode.
P006: Introduces the rationale for testing under extremely rapid fluctuations to mimic real-world renewable energy intermittency.
P007: Reports exceptional stability of NiCoP–Cr₂O₃ under 10-minute start–shutdown cycles for 4,500 h and at high current densities (3–10 A cm⁻²), culminating in a >1,000-hour stable AEM electrolyser demonstration under realistic conditions.
