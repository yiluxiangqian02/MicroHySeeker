Section Role: This section presents the Introduction and core findings of the study.  
Core Content: The study identifies cathode degradation during intermittent alkaline seawater electrolysis due to oxidation and halide adsorption during shutdown cycles, and demonstrates that a NiCoP–Cr₂O₃ cathode with an in situ-formed phosphate passivation layer enables stable operation at 0.5 A cm⁻² for 10,000 h (voltage increase rate <0.5% khr⁻¹), 1 A cm⁻² for 4,500 h under 10-min start–shutdown cycles, and 10 A cm⁻² for 700 h (<1.8% khr⁻¹).  
Key Entities: NiCoP–Cr₂O₃ cathode, phosphate passivation layer, alkaline seawater, intermittent electrolysis, start–shutdown cycles, 0.5 A cm⁻², 1 A cm⁻², 10 A cm⁻², 10,000 h, 4,500 h, 700 h, voltage increase rate <0.5% khr⁻¹, voltage increase rate <1.8% khr⁻¹, halide ions (Cl⁻, Br⁻)
