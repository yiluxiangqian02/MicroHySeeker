Section Positioning: This section investigates cathode oxidation and corrosion phenomena occurring during intermittent (start–shutdown) water electrolysis cycles and introduces a catalyst design strategy to mitigate such degradation.

Paragraph Index:
P001: Describes the experimental setup for simulating start–shutdown water electrolysis using a NiCoP–Cr₂O₃ cathode and Ni foam anode at 1 A cm⁻², referencing supplementary data.
P002: Presents Figure 1, which schematically illustrates cathode oxidation/corrosion during cycling and provides quantitative electrochemical data showing rising cathode potentials (up to 1.30 V vs. RHE) after shutdown.
P003: Directs readers to supplementary material (Note 1 and Fig. 2a,b) containing voltage–time (V–t) and current–time (I–t) plots corresponding to the cycling experiments.
P004: Details the methodology for quantitative monitoring of cathode potential using a Hg/HgO reference electrode, reports observed cathodic discharge behavior post-shutdown, and explains how this leads to oxidative damage exceeding the thermodynamic water oxidation potential—especially exacerbated in seawater due to halide accumulation.
P005: Introduces a new subsection titled “Catalysts design strategy,” signaling a shift from problem description to solution development.
P006: Explains the rationale for using phosphorus in catalyst design, highlighting its multiple oxidation states and ability to form oxygen-rich, protective passivation layers when combined with metal oxides.
P007: Elaborates on the role of Cr₂O₃ as a stable, high-potential-resistant oxide that enhances passivation layer density and blocks transmembrane oxygen; concludes by presenting the integrated design—phosphorization plus Cr₂O₃ coating—to protect NiCo-based HER cathodes during cyclic operation, supported by supplementary figures.
