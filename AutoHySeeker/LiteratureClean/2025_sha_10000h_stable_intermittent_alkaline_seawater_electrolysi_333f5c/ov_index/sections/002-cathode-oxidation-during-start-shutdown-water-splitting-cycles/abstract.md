Section Role: This section presents experimental results and analysis of cathode oxidation during intermittent water electrolysis cycles.  
Core Content: Using a NiCoP–Cr₂O₃ cathode and Ni foam anode in simulated start–shutdown water/seawater electrolysis at 0.5–1 A cm⁻² over 12–72 h cycles, the study demonstrates that post-shutdown cathodic discharge drives the cathode potential to 1.09–1.30 V versus RHE—exceeding the 1.23 V thermodynamic threshold for water oxidation—causing irreversible oxidative damage and halide-induced corrosion, particularly in seawater.  
Key Entities: NiCoP–Cr₂O₃ cathode, Ni foam anode, start–shutdown cycles, 0.5–1 A cm⁻², 12–72 h, 1.09–1.30 V vs. RHE, 1.23 V vs. RHE (water oxidation threshold), seawater, halide anions, chronopotentiometric electrolysis, HgO reference electrode, phosphate passivation layer, Cr₂O₃ heterostructure
