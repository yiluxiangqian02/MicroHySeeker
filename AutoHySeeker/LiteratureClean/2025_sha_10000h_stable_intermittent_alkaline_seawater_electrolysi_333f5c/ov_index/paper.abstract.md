Research Object: NiCoP–Cr₂O₃ cathode for intermittent alkaline seawater electrolysis.  
Core Finding: The catalyst enables stable hydrogen evolution at 0.5 A cm⁻² for 10,000 h in alkaline seawater with a voltage increase rate of only 0.5% khr⁻¹, and maintains performance under harsher conditions including 1 A cm⁻² for 4,500 h (10-min start–shutdown cycles) and 10 A cm⁻² for 700 h with <1.8% khr⁻¹ degradation.  
Method: An in situ-formed phosphate passivation layer on NiCoP–Cr₂O₃ was engineered to suppress cathode oxidation and halide adsorption during intermittent operation, validated through chronopotentiometry, TOF-SIMS, and long-term stability testing under simulated renewable energy fluctuations.  
Conclusion: This work identifies cathode degradation during intermittent seawater electrolysis as a critical challenge and demonstrates a passivation-layer strategy that enables unprecedented operational stability, advancing practical green hydrogen production from seawater using renewable electricity.  
Keywords: NiCoP–Cr₂O₃, alkaline seawater electrolysis, hydrogen evolution reaction, passivation layer, intermittent operation, voltage stability, chloride corrosion resistance, TOF-SIMS
