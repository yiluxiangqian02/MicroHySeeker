Paper Positioning: This paper investigates the dependence of reverse current on the electrode surface in a bipolar-type alkaline water electrolyzer, identifying active nickel-based materials as the dominant driver and distinguishing their contribution from that of dissolved gases.

Section Index:
  001-dependence-of-the-reverse-current-on-the-surface-of-electrode-pl: Dependence of the Reverse Current on the Surface of Electrode Placed on a Bipolar Plate in an Alkaline Water Electrolyzer  
  002-1-introduction: 1. Introduction – Introduces the context of renewable energy integration, hydrogen production via alkaline water electrolysis, and the problem of reverse and leak currents in bipolar electrolyzers, emphasizing electrode degradation mechanisms.  
  003-2-experimental: 2. Experimental – Describes a two-cell bipolar alkaline electrolyzer setup with Ni mesh electrodes, Nafion separators, controlled electrolyte flow, and a protocol to replace post-electrolysis electrolyte with gas-free NaOH to isolate surface redox effects; includes equivalent circuit modeling and measurement methods for leak/reverse currents and cell voltages.  
  004-3-results-and-discussion: 3. Results and Discussion – Presents cell voltage characteristics at low current densities, validates a simplified equivalent circuit model for leak current prediction, and analyzes post-electrolysis voltage behavior under electrolyte replacement, linking reverse current to nickel-based redox couples rather than dissolved H₂/O₂.  
  005-4-conclusions: 4. Conclusions – Summarizes that the primary source of reverse current is the electrochemically active nickel oxide/hydroxide species on the electrode surface, not gaseous reductants/oxidants, and highlights implications for mitigating electrode degradation in alkaline electrolyzers.
