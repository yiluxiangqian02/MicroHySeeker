Section Positioning: This section introduces the motivation for alkaline water electrolysis as a solution to renewable energy intermittency and outlines key operational challenges—specifically leak and reverse currents—in bipolar-type systems.

Paragraph Index:  
P001: Establishes the context by linking CO₂-driven global warming to the need for renewable energy integration, highlights intermittency issues, and positions hydrogen production via water electrolysis—particularly alkaline systems—as a promising storage and transport solution due to cost and durability advantages.  
P002: Describes the structural and electrical configuration of alkaline water electrolyzers, distinguishing monopolar from bipolar types, and introduces the concept of leak currents through ionic conduction in manifolds, noting their negative impacts on efficiency and material corrosion.  
P003: Explains how reverse current flowing through the bipolar plate during shutdown causes electrode degradation via unintended reduction or oxidation reactions, emphasizing the need for mitigation strategies.

P005: States the specific objective of the present study—to investigate leak and reverse currents in bipolar alkaline electrolyzers—with a focus on quantifying contributions from dissolved gases and surface oxidation states to the total reverse current.
