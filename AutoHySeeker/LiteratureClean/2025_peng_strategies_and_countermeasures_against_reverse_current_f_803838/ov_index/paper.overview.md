Paper Positioning: This paper provides a comprehensive review of the mechanisms, origins, and detrimental effects of reverse current in water electrolysis systems—particularly under intermittent renewable energy operation—and critically evaluates state-of-the-art mitigation strategies spanning materials engineering to system-level controls to enhance electrolyzer durability.

Section Index:

  002-1-introduction: 1. Introduction – Introduces green hydrogen production via water electrolysis, highlights the challenge posed by renewable intermittency-induced reverse current, and outlines its impact across ALK, PEM, and AEM technologies.  
  003-2-background-of-reverse-current: 2. Background of Reverse Current – Explains the electrochemical origins of reverse current during shutdown or power fluctuations, discusses its prevalence in large-scale stacks, and identifies gaps in mechanistic understanding.  
  004-3-mitigation-strategies-against-reverse-current: 3. Mitigation Strategies Against Reverse Current – Reviews material-level approaches (e.g., doped Ni-based cathodes, protective coatings), operational adjustments (e.g., electrolyte composition, temperature), and system-level solutions (e.g., polarization rectifiers, intelligent control circuits).  
  005-4-conclusion-and-perspective: 4. Conclusion and Perspective – Summarizes key insights on reverse current degradation and mitigation, and proposes future research directions including dynamic protective layers and in situ/operando characterization for durable electrolyzer design.
