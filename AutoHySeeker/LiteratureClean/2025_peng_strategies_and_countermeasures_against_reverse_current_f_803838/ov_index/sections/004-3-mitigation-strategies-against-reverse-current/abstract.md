Section Role: This section presents mitigation strategies against reverse current in water electrolyzers, focusing on material engineering and external environmental regulation approaches.  
Core Content: The section evaluates multiple strategies to suppress reverse current damage, testing materials including Pb-doped Ni (showing 2.3% vs. ~10% current loss after start-stop cycling), WMoCoP@NM (stable under 1.5/0.4 A cm⁻² cycling and SU/SD tests), NiMoN@NC-6/NF (pyrolyzed at 600°C, stable in acid/alkaline SU/SD tests with minimal voltage drift), RuO₂/Ni (susceptible to RuO₄²⁻ dissolution), and NiCoP–Cr₂O₃ (enabling 10,000-h seawater electrolysis stability), alongside external methods like Pb sacrificial anodes (0.02 mV·dec Tafel slope change) and polarized rectifiers, under conditions such as 85°C, 30 wt.% KOH, 0.5 mol L⁻¹ H₂SO₄, and cyclic voltage switching (4.5 V ↔ 0 V).
