Section Role: This section provides a comprehensive background on the origin, mechanisms, and detrimental impacts of reverse current in electrochemical water electrolysis systems, with a focus on material-specific degradation pathways and testing methodologies.  
Core Content: The section details how reverse current—triggered by power interruptions or fluctuations in AEM and PEM electrolyzers—induces cathode oxidation (e.g., Ni → Ni(OH)₂ → NiOOH in alkaline systems; Pt/C corrosion at >1.5 V vs RHE in acidic systems) and anode reduction (e.g., IrO₂ → metallic Ir), leading to catalyst dissolution, structural collapse, energy waste, and safety hazards such as H₂/O₂ recombination; it also outlines key evaluation methods including reverse-current chronopotentiometry (monitoring potential stability under fixed reverse current), cyclic voltammetry stress testing (45k SU/SD cycles), and post-mortem characterization via SEM, XRD, and XPS.  
Key Entities: nickel (Ni), Ni-Mo, Ni-Fe, platinum (Pt), iridium dioxide (IrO₂), titanium dioxide (TiO₂), anion exchange membrane (AEM), proton exchange membrane (PEM), reverse-current chronopotentiometry, cyclic voltammetry, SEM, XRD, XPS, 1.5 V vs RHE, 45k startup/shutdown cycles, Raman peaks at 447 and 504 cm⁻¹, β-Ni(OH)₂, γ-NiOOH, carbon corrosion, hydrogen/oxygen recombination
