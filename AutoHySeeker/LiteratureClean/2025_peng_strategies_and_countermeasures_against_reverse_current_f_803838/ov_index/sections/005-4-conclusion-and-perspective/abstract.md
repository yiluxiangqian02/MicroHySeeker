Section Role: This section serves as the Conclusion and Perspective, synthesizing key challenges and proposing future research directions for addressing reverse current in anion exchange membrane water electrolysis (AEMWE).  
Core Content: The section identifies reverse current—driven by galvanic cell formation during intermittent renewable energy operation—as a critical cause of nickel cathode corrosion (forming Ni(OH)₂/NiO passivation layers), leading to increased hydrogen evolution overpotential and performance decay; it reviews current mitigation strategies (e.g., Fe/Co/Mo/Cr doping, core-shell nanostructures, polarization rectifiers) and advocates for a paradigm shift toward AI-driven high-throughput screening, DFT-guided design, multifunctional dynamic protective layers (e.g., graphene, conductive metal oxides), and operando diagnostics (EIS, XAS, Raman) for real-time health monitoring and intelligent control.
