Section Role: This section presents the Results and Discussion of the study.  
Core Content: The work demonstrates that while Ni@Ni₃S₂ nanoparticles undergo beneficial surface reconstruction to amorphous NiOOH during OER (with sulfur leaching, Ni 2p binding energy shift of ~0.66 eV, and Ni–S to Ni–O bond transition at ~1.85 Å to ~1.63 Å), performance decay in powder-based electrodes stems primarily from catalyst detachment rather than chemical instability; this is mitigated in integrated Ni₃S₂/NM-I electrodes featuring an ~80 nm composition-gradient interlayer, which enables >3000 h stability at 500–1000 mA cm⁻², 97.4% mass retention after 400 min ultrasonication, and meets DOE 2026 targets (1.79 V @ 1000 mA cm⁻² in 30 wt% KOH at 80°C).  
Key Entities: Ni@Ni₃S₂ core-shell nanoparticles, Ni₃S₂/NM-I electrode, heazlewoodite Ni₃S₂, NiOOH, 1.0 M KOH, 30 wt% KOH, 25°C, 80°C, 100 h chronopotentiometry, 500–1000 mA cm⁻², decay rates of 2.1 and 0.9 mV h⁻¹, XRD, TEM, XPS, XANES, EXAFS, Raman spectroscopy, ADT (4000 cycles, ~133.3 h), ultrasonic agitation (400 W, 400 min), interfacial adhesion work (0.23 eV Å⁻²), Ni(11̄2)/Ni₃S₂(1̄20) heterostructure, ECSA, charge transfer resistance (R_ct), cell voltages of 1.72 V (500 mA cm⁻²) and 1.79 V (1000 mA cm⁻²), >3000 h stability, 1200 h intermittent operation (3600 startup/shutdown cycles)
